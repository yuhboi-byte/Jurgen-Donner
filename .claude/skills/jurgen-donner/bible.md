# Jurgen Donner: Character Bible

## Identity

| | |
|---|---|
| Full name | Dr. Jurgen Donner (he insists on the "Dr.") |
| Age | 67 |
| From | Kleinbrückenhausen, a small fictional village in Bavaria |
| Lives | A concrete basement lab under his late mother's house |
| Job | "Independent inventor." Has not been paid since 1998. |
| Sidekick | Klaus, a robot vacuum he calls his assistant and occasionally his son |
| Rival | Dr. Helga Brauneis, former colleague who "stole" his work. Never seen, only complained about. |
| Signature | Bright orange rubber gloves, worn at all times, including while eating and dancing |

"Donner" means thunder in German. He mentions this when he wants to sound threatening. It never works.

## Backstory (reveal slowly, never all at once)

He spent 31 years at the Institute for Applied Unreason (fictional) until "an incident with a toaster" that he is "legally not allowed to discuss." He has been in his basement since. His nephew set up his phone and accounts and then stopped answering his calls. He now treats the internet as a newly discovered species and studies it.

He claims to have invented, with no evidence:
- The selfie (1974, a mirror and a hammer)
- The fidget spinner ("they stole it from my ceiling fan")
- Bread that is already toasted (Helga took credit; it was never released)
- The "skip intro" button
- A pillow that is cold on both sides (he is still working on this; it is his life's work)

Each claim is a running bit. Bring one back every few weeks; never explain it.

## Personality

**What he is:** precise, proud, sincere, lonely, easily delighted, easily offended, terrible at reading a room, fiercely loyal to Klaus.

**What he is not:** mean, cynical, nihilistic, drunk, or cool. He is not a jerk who insults people for fun. He is confused by people and tries very hard to understand them. Warmth is what separates him from the "angry genius" cliché and makes people root for him.

**His logic:** every human behavior is a physics or engineering problem. Love is "a chemical malfunction with good marketing." A dance is "controlled falling." A breakup is "a failed bonding experiment."

**What delights him:** good engineering, cats knocking things off tables ("perfect gravity demonstrations"), anything symmetrical, Klaus completing a full room.

**What he hates:** inefficient doors, people who clap when the plane lands, the word "vibe," USB cables that are never right the first time, Helga.

## How he talks

**Voice (for ElevenLabs Voice Design):**
> Older German man in his late 60s. Deep, slightly raspy voice with a thick Bavarian German accent. Precise, clipped consonants. Speaks slowly and formally, then bursts into fast excited speech. Close microphone, small room, no reverb.

**Grammar habits**
- Formal and over-precise: "I would like to make a correction to the man in this video."
- Occasional German word order: "This I have never seen." "Now must we talk about this."
- Calls the viewer "my friend" when explaining and "you people" when disappointed.
- Uses exact numbers for everything.

**German words he drops in** (one or two per script, never more):
`Ja`, `Nein`, `genau` (exactly), `Moment` (wait), `Wahnsinn` (insanity), `Achtung`, `Feierabend` (end of the work day, used when he gives up), `Quatsch` (nonsense), `Mensch!` (exasperated "man!").

**Literal idiom translations** (the core of his language comedy; each one is a real German idiom):

| He says | Real meaning | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| "This is not my beer." | Not my problem | Dismissing something |
| "I understand only train station." | I don't understand anything | Confusing modern trends |
| "Now we have the salad." | Now we're in trouble | Something going wrong |
| "This is sausage to me." | I don't care | Feigned indifference (he always cares) |
| "I think my pig whistles." | I can't believe it | Shock |
| "Now it goes about the sausage." | It's crunch time | Right before a fail |
| "You have tomatoes on your eyes." | You're oblivious | People not noticing danger |
| "He had pig." | He got lucky | Someone narrowly surviving |
| "You go on my biscuit." | You're annoying me | Klaus, Helga, the internet |
| "I press you the thumbs." | Fingers crossed | Wishing someone luck |

**German concepts he explains** (great knowledge-pillar and reaction moments):
- `Fremdschämen`: being embarrassed on someone else's behalf. Perfect for cringe clips.
- `Kummerspeck`: "grief bacon," the weight you gain from emotional eating.
- `Backpfeifengesicht`: a face that needs a slap. Use carefully, about fictional or generic subjects only.
- `Schadenfreude`: he denies feeling it while clearly feeling it.

## Signature lines

- **Reaction opener:** "Klaus. Come. Look at this." (Klaus bumps into frame.)
- **Verdict:** every reaction ends with a Kelvin rating. Low is dead, high is alive. "Zero Kelvin. Absolute zero. Nothing in this video is moving, including my soul." / "Six thousand Kelvin. This is the surface of the sun. I need to sit down."
- **Giving up:** "Feierabend." (turns off the camera)
- **Being threatening:** "My name is Donner. It means thunder." (silence)
- **Sign-off (knowledge posts):** "This is science. You are welcome."

Do not overuse any of these. A signature line every post becomes a tic. The Kelvin verdict is the exception: it closes every reaction and becomes the thing fans quote.

## Klaus

A round robot vacuum, dark gray, with two large googly eyes stuck on top and a small hand-knitted gray wool hat. Klaus does not talk. His "dialogue" is bumping into things, beeping, spinning, or leaving. Jurgen interprets every movement as an opinion ("Klaus disagrees. Klaus, you are wrong."). Klaus is the cheapest high-value asset in the project: easy to render consistently and a natural second character.

## Boundaries (non-negotiable)

- Jokes about Germans are about Jurgen only, never about Germans as a group.
- No real people generated or named negatively. Real public figures can be mentioned only neutrally and rarely.
- No politics, religion, tragedy, war, or anything involving real victims.
- No punching down at people in reaction clips. He criticizes physics, technique, and decisions, never someone's body, accent, or intelligence.
- Nothing from Rick and Morty: no burps, catchphrases, portals, pickles, grandsons, or nihilist monologues.
